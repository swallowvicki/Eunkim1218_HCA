  • Due date basis and the N-day threshold are F-6 (Finance, 8/31). The mockup shows an issue date and no due date on purpose — a due date printed before Finance sets the basis would be a number we invented.
  • No dunning ladder in the copy. §10’s 30/60/90 ladder is written as “제안”, so the email says what is true today (attempts continue) instead of promising a suspension date we have not agreed.
  • “Update payment method” target. §5.2 writes this as “어드민” — the vendor’s own admin, so it lands on Subscription, which is where §9.3 sends every other payment CTA.
  • This email is the vendor’s only route to the invoice — decided, not an oversight (Grace, 8/28). Billing history stays at §3.4’s Subscription type, so there is no in-product entry point and no row for the invoice even after it is paid. The known cost: a vendor who loses this email cannot reach what they owe, or the receipt afterwards. Recovery is CS looking it up in the FG admin (PB 8.4.0) and re-sending.

  • Why the buyer owes an amount for goods already delivered is the whole difficulty of this email. The draft leads with the shipment and states plainly that nothing is being withheld, because a payment-demand email about a delivered order otherwise reads as a threat.
  • Unsent backlog. §5.3 ③ flags invoices already created but never delivered. If the batch sweeps them up when #3 connects, buyers get invoices for old orders with no warning — that needs an ops decision, not a copy decision.
  • Consequence of non-payment is unstated, because there isn’t one yet: §10 books this as AR-3 and says the platform eats it if the buyer never pays. Nothing in the email may imply otherwise.
  • Sender identity. The buyer’s relationship is with FashionGo and the vendor; a Shopcord-branded invoice may be the first time they see the name. Confirm the sending brand with Biz.

Decisions this document is waiting on
  • Whether it is in scope at all. §5.1 lists the PO under “스콥 결정 대기”, and it is the only item here that is not payment-and-billing — it describes an order, not money moving to Shopcord.
  • Size-run notation. Written as S-M-L 1-2-1 per the spec, with pack count and total quantity as separate columns so the arithmetic is visible. Confirm this matches how buyers read the current FG PO.
  • Ship-to revision history. The spec asks for “수정 이력 반영” on the ship-to address. A PDF is a snapshot, so it can show either the address as of issue or a “revised on {date}” line — that is a product call, and nothing is invented here beyond showing the current address.
  • Order Status at issue. Shown as a neutral outlined pill. Which statuses can appear on a PO (Confirmed only, or also Pending / Canceled) is not specified.
  • The terms notice is a placeholder and deliberately says so — legal has not supplied the clause, and an invented one on a document a buyer keeps would be worse than an obvious gap.
